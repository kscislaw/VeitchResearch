Veitch Index
Index to Register of Deeds

Prior to the formalization of the centralized Register of Deeds in 1660, private contracts, financial obligations, and legal agreements were recorded across a patchwork of central, local, and ecclesiastical registries. (Actual heritable land transfers are found in Registers of Sasines, not in Registers of Deeds)

Central Court Registers

Books of Council and Session: The principal central registry, operating under the Court of Session from its foundation in 1532 (with formal registration for preservation and execution beginning around 1554).

Acts of Sederunt / Register of Acts and Decreets: Recorded the judicial decisions and enforcement orders of the Lords of Council and Session, often containing transcribed obligations and agreements.

Books of the Privy Council (Register of the Privy Council): Frequently registered bonds of caution (bails/sureties), peace agreements between clans or families, and state-level private obligations.

Local and Regional Registers

Burgh Court Books (or Register of Deeds of the Burgh): Maintained by individual Royal Burghs to record local trade agreements, debts, apprenticeships, and merchant contracts.

Sheriff Court Books: Kept by the sheriffdoms to record bonds, obligations, and civil disputes between non-burgesses within the county.

Commissary Court Books (post-1560): Established after the Reformation to take over the civil jurisdiction of the old church courts, registering testaments, bonds, and slander disputes.

Regality and Barony Court Books: Kept by local feudal lords who held jurisdiction over specific estates (regalities or baronies), recording agreements and debts among tenants and vassals.

Pre-Reformation & Transitional Registers

Protocol Books of Notaries Public: Before official central registers existed, individuals relied on public notaries to draft and record legal transactions. A notary's personal protocol book served as the primary legal record of private agreements, bonds, and early property transfers.

Official Court Books of the Bishops (Consistory Court Books): Pre-1560 ecclesiastical registers that recorded oaths, debt obligations, and matrimonial agreements enforced under canon law.
