Index to Particular Register of Sasines for Sheriffdoms of Edinburgh,
Haddington, Linlithgow and Bathgate

The Register of Sasines was established by an Act of the Parliament of Scotland in 1617 to create a permanent, public, and central recording system for all land transactions. By requiring all deeds, legal transfers, and mortgages (bonds) to be registered within 60 days of the physical sasine ceremony, the system prevented fraudulent double-selling of land and established clear legal title.

The Particular Registers were kept at the regional/sheriffdom level (unlike the General Register kept in Edinburgh for the entire country), capturing local transactions within specific boundaries.

  • Entries routinely specify family connections to establish legal rights to property—listing fathers, sons, spouses, brothers, and relict (widow) status (e.g., "sister of John V., burgess of Musselburgh, and spouse of Alexander Druppis").
  • The full underlying register entries detail specific tenements, acres, houses, boundaries, rights to water or grazing, and the former owners of the land.
  • They document mortgages, marriage jointures (property settlements for wives), debts secured against land, and patterns of wealth accumulation within merchant families and local gentry.
  • Sasines reveal when a family member inherited land upon a relative's death (via a Precept of Clare Constat) or when individuals moved between parishes or towns while retaining property rights elsewhere.
